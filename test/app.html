      Verifiable Machine Learning (VML) provides provable guarantees for ML systems through
                                 a three-axis taxonomy covering verification objectives, technical methods, and architectural
                                 paradigms. The survey analyzes 222 papers from 2010 to August 2026, organizing work
                                 around four objectives: computational integrity of inference, training, and aggregation;
                                 property and behavior conformance including robustness and fairness; data and model
                                 integrity; and ownership verification. Cryptographic proofs account for roughly half
                                 the corpus, while formal methods address robustness and safety verification. The field
                                 balances strong mathematical guarantees against practical scalability, involves unavoidable
                                 trust assumptions, and faces challenges in generalizability, privacy protection, and
                                 real-world deployment despite rapid advancement driven by demand for trustworthy AI
                                 systems.

      Machine learning practitioners perceive sustainability through two primary lenses:
                                 as efficiency-performance trade-offs or ethical responsibility. This mixed-method
                                 study involving eight interviews and 203 survey respondents reveals significant disconnects
                                 between sustainability awareness and systematic implementation. Practitioners primarily
                                 adopt environmental sustainability approaches like CPU/GPU optimization and cloud
                                 technologies, while social and economic dimensions receive limited attention. Key
                                 challenges include lack of standardized metrics, absent integration methods, insufficient
                                 organizational support, and inadequate tooling. The study identifies nineteen sustainability
                                 approaches across five categories and fourteen barriers hindering adoption. Findings
                                 emphasize sustainability should be treated as a measurable non-functional requirement
                                 integrated throughout the ML development lifecycle rather than an afterthought.

      This paper presents symbolic basic block profiling, a static analysis technique that
                                 generates symbolic expressions for basic block execution counts in machine learning
                                 kernels rather than requiring dynamic instrumentation. The method statically analyzes
                                 programs to derive formulae representing block execution frequencies as functions
                                 of input parameters like tensor dimensions. Implemented in LLVM, the technique successfully
                                 handles 73 of 78 ML operators from TVM, achieving exact profiles with median speedup
                                 of 15093x compared to instrumentation-based profiling. The approach uses a graph-based
                                 representation with operational semantics to handle loops, branches, and early exits.
                                 While limited to shape-driven countable kernels, symbolic profiling enables efficient
                                 profile extraction across different input sizes without program execution overhead,
                                 facilitating performance modeling and kernel auto-tuning applications.
